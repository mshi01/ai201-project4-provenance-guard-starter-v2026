## What This Does

<!-- The path a submission takes, from arriving to the label a reader sees.
     Six or seven steps — this is the trace you wrote in Milestone 1. -->
This app, Provenance Guard, analyzes submitted text to estimate whether it was written by a person or generated by AI.

## Signals and Scoring

<!-- For each signal: what property it measures, why that might differ between
     human and AI writing, and WHAT IT CAN'T SEE.

     The blind spot is not optional. If you can't name it you don't understand
     the signal yet — and unit 8's attack set is built out of blind spots. -->

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:**

It runs a language model on your own machine and asks one question: **how predictable is this text?**. It measures **perplexity**. Low perplexity means "I could have guessed this"; high perplexity means "that surprised me".

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:**

A language model writes by repeatedly picking a likely next word, so text a model produced tends
to be text a model finds easy to predict. Human writing wanders more — odd word
choices, abrupt turns, the specific rather than the typical. 

**What it can't see:**

Predictability is not the same thing as authorship. What this signal actually rewards is **common words in short, ordinary sentences** — and plenty of real people write that way.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:**

This one measures it *as text* — the shape of it, without understanding a word.
It performed three measures: 
- sentence_length_spread   do sentences vary in length, or march in step?
- type_token_ratio         how much of the vocabulary repeats?
- punctuation_density      how much punctuation, and how varied?

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:**

Generated prose tends toward even, mid-length sentences and a narrower band of vocabulary, because
that's what "most likely next word" produces on average. Human writing is often more varied. 

**What it can't see:**

This signal does not read the text meaning. 

### The combining rule

<!-- Write the rule in words, then give the numbers. What happens when the two
     signals disagree? -->

**The rule:**

The final score will be a weighted score of the model signal and the style signal.The model signal will have more weights.


**The numbers:**

WEIGHT_MODEL_SIGNAL = 0.7
WEIGHT_STYLE_SIGNAL = 0.3

The model signal measures predictability and is generally more meaningful than surface style. Stylometry is easier to manipulate with punctuation, sentence splitting, typos, or quotations, so giving it less weight reduces false accusations.

HUMAN_THRESHOLD = 0.30
AI_THRESHOLD = 0.75

The higher AI threshold requires stronger evidence before labeling text as AI.

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

<!-- ⚠️ The grader checks your code against that line. If your rule lives
     somewhere else, say where. -->

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:**

The model gives more weights to model signal than style signal as predictablility is generally more meaningful than style.

## Label Variants

<!-- The exact text of all three labels, as a reader would see them, and the
     score range each covers.

     Write them for someone who has never heard the word "threshold". -->

| Label | Score range | The exact text a reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| high-confidence human | 0 - 0.30 | We think this was probably written by a person. |
| unsure | 0.30 - 0.75 | We cannot tell confidently whether this was written by a person or by AI. |
| high-confidence AI | 0.75 - 1.0 | We think this was probably written by AI. |

**Why I worded the "unsure" one this way:**
<!-- It's the hardest of the three. It has to admit uncertainty without
     sounding like an accusation, because the person reading it may well have
     written every word themselves. -->
This widens the unsure range to 0.30–0.75, reducing false accusations from uncertain or ordinary human writing. The higher AI threshold requires stronger evidence before labeling text as AI.

## How I Used AI

**Moment 1**

- *What I asked for:*

I asked for suggestions on setting the score thresholds for identifying human- and AI-written text.

- *What came back:*

The human threshold is relatively low, so most texts fall between the human and AI thresholds and receive an unsure label.

- *What I changed:*

I suggest slightly increasing the human threshold because incorrectly labeling human-written text as AI has a higher cost than failing to identify some AI-written text.

**Moment 2**

- *What I asked for:*

I asked for a suggested reference value for calculating the mesure of low_punctuation in stylometry.py. It suggest 0.1. 

- *What came back:*

However, because punctuation marks make up a relatively small proportion of the text, the calculated low_punctuation value is lower than the other two measurements.

- *What I changed:*

I would like to increase the reference value in calculating the low_punctuation.
